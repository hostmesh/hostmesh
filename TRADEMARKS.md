# Trademark policy

Hostmesh is open source: its code is licensed under the [Apache License, Version 2.0](LICENSE), and anyone may use, change and redistribute it. The license covers the code. It does not cover the name. Section 6 of the license says so: it grants no permission to use the licensor's trade names, trademarks, service marks or product names, except as needed to describe where the work comes from.

This policy says what you may do with the name, so that "Hostmesh" keeps meaning one thing: the software published by the Hostmesh project at <https://github.com/hostmesh/hostmesh>.

## The marks

- The name **Hostmesh**, in any capitalization.
- Any logo the project publishes for itself. There is none yet. When there is one, this policy covers it.
- The names of the project's packages and namespaces where they contain the name: the `hostmesh` vendor on Packagist, the `@hostmesh` scope on npm, Python projects that start with `hostmesh`, and the `hostmesh` namespace on container registries.

The marks belong to the Hostmesh Authors. They are not registered trademarks.

## What you may do without asking

- **Refer to the project by its name**, truthfully: "we run Hostmesh", "a talk about Hostmesh", "how Hostmesh places accounts on nodes".
- **Say that your work relates to it**, when that is true: "compatible with Hostmesh", "a theme for Hostmesh", "an add-on for Hostmesh", "built on Hostmesh", "a fork of Hostmesh".
- **Run it under your own brand.** Hostmesh is built for this: an operator sets a product name, logos and colors, and customers see those. You need not mention Hostmesh to them, and you may if you wish ("powered by Hostmesh").
- **Distribute it unchanged** under its name: packages for a distribution, container images, mirrors. Unchanged includes the changes a package needs in order to build and install; say that the package is yours and not the project's.
- **Offer services around it**: hosting, support, consulting and training "for Hostmesh".

In all of these, use the name as a name. Do not make it more prominent than your own name, and do not style it as your logo.

## What needs permission

- **Naming your own product, service, company or project "Hostmesh"**, or a name that could be confused with it: "Hostmesh Pro", "Hostmesh Cloud", "OpenHostmesh", "Hostmash".
- **Distributing a changed version under the name.** If you change the code and give the result to others, give it another name. You may, and should, say what it is based on ("based on Hostmesh"). This is what keeps a bug in a fork from being reported as a bug in Hostmesh. A version you change and run only for yourself is not affected.
- **Suggesting that the project endorses, sponsors or is behind** your product, service or event.
- **Using the name in a domain name, a package name, a registry namespace or an account name** on a social network or a code host.
- **Putting the name or a project logo on merchandise** for sale.
- **Changing a project logo**, or combining it with your own.

## Asking for permission

Open an issue in <https://github.com/hostmesh/hostmesh/issues> and say what you want to do and where. Community uses that are clearly not commercial, such as a user group or a meetup, will usually get a quick yes.

If you see the name used in a way that confuses people about where software comes from, tell us the same way.
